Built for scrutiny, not just storage

Five things happen automatically to every document that enters OneRoofTraceability — none of them need a spreadsheet on the side.

Audit trail

Every create, edit, review and approval is timestamped and attributed. An assessor can trace one requirement from elicitation to qualification test without leaving the system.

Version control

Every record carries a visible version tag. Superseded versions are archived, never deleted, so the current baseline and its history sit side by side.

Reviewers

Each work product routes to a named technical and quality reviewer. Nothing moves to the next stage until the right person has signed off.

Workflow

Classification, routing and approval gates run automatically. People are only pulled in for the review steps that genuinely need a human judgment call.

Access restrictions

Permissions follow role and discipline. Mechanical engineers see mechanical records by default; who can read, edit or approve is set per group, not per file.

Automotive SPICE® capability levels required

Every process box in the V-Cycle below (SYS, SWE, HWE, SUP, MAN and more) can be assessed against six capability levels, as defined in the Automotive SPICE® v4.1 Process Assessment Model (VDA QMC). Levels 2–5 are cumulative — each one requires the level below it to be fully achieved. Pick a level to see what it expects and how that maps to a document's status in OneRoofTraceability.

1

Performed process

PA 1.1 — Process performance
Purpose achieved Base practices carried out Outputs exist

The process delivers on its intended purpose: the defined base practices (e.g. eliciting requirements, designing an architecture, running verification) are actually carried out and produce the expected work products. At this level the question is simply whether the work got done — not yet how well it is planned, reviewed or repeated.

Document maturity & status

Work products exist and cover the required content, but formal planning, review cycles and baselines are inconsistent or missing. Records are commonly still being written or awaiting their first pass.

Typically Draft → In Work
2

Managed process

PA 2.1 Performance management · PA 2.2 Work product management
Planned & monitored Resourced Work products controlled Reviewed against criteria

The performed process from Level 1 is now managed: it is planned, resourced, monitored against that plan and adjusted when needed. Its work products are properly identified, documented, reviewed against defined criteria, and placed under change and configuration control rather than left to individual habit.

Document maturity & status

Documents carry a named owner and reviewer, move through a real review cycle, and are version-controlled once approved. This is where a work product first earns a formal, auditable status transition.

Typically Under Review → Final, version-locked
3

Established process

PA 3.1 Process definition · PA 3.2 Process deployment
Standard process Tailored from org assets Consistently deployed

The managed process is now built from a defined, organization-wide standard process, tailored to the specific project, and deployed consistently across projects — not reinvented each time. Roles, competencies and infrastructure needed to run it are in place organization-wide.

Document maturity & status

Documents follow shared templates and naming conventions so a Final work product looks and behaves the same way on every project, making audits and cross-project comparison straightforward.

Final, produced from a standard organizational template
4

Predictable process

PA 4.1 Quantitative analysis · PA 4.2 Quantitative control
Quantitative targets Measured & analyzed Variation controlled

The established process now operates predictably within defined quantitative limits. Measurement data is collected and analyzed to identify the assignable causes behind variation, and corrective action is taken to keep the process performing within its expected range.

Document maturity & status

Final work products are accompanied by measurement records — trend data, defect rates, review effectiveness — that demonstrate the process is hitting its targets consistently, not just that a reviewer signed off once.

Final, backed by quantitative process measures
5

Innovating process

PA 5.1 Process innovation · PA 5.2 Innovation implementation
Continuous improvement Root-cause driven Responds to org change

The predictable process is now continually improved. Improvement opportunities are identified from the quantitative data gathered at Level 4 and from innovative ideas and technologies, then implemented in a controlled way so the process keeps responding to changing organizational goals.

Document maturity & status

Lessons captured from Final, archived documents feed back into updated organizational templates and criteria, so the next project's baseline starts from an already-improved standard rather than the same starting point.

Final, feeding continuous improvement of the org's templates
